# Educational Storyboarding Skill

## Six-Scene Structure

Organize every ~5-minute lesson into six progressive scenes:
1. **Hook / Context** (~25–30 s) — Motivate with a visual question or concrete puzzle.
2. **Concept Introduction** (~30–35 s) — Define notation, objects, and formal rule clearly.
3. **Visual Intuition** (~60–65 s) — Build geometric/visual intuition before dense algebra.
4. **Worked Example** (~60–85 s) — Walk step by step through one carefully chosen example without skipping intermediate calculations.
5. **Interpretation** (~60–75 s) — Connect the numerical result back to geometry, scaling, or structural meaning.
6. **Summary & Credits** (~30–35 s) — Recap three concise takeaways, followed by references, music credit, and AI disclosure.

## Pacing Guidelines

- Target **500–560 spoken words** (~140–150 wpm active narration) plus deliberate visual observation pauses to reach **~4:55–5:05** (strict maximum: **7:00**).
- Group narration into **15–22 short semantic beats** (1–3 sentences each) so every visual action pairs cleanly with speech.
- Never let a scene become a static definition slide — every scene requires purposeful mathematical motion.
